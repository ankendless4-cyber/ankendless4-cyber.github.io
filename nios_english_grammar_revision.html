Quick grammar reminders

Since + starting point: since Monday. For + duration: for two days.
After did / does, use the base verb (V1): “Did she go?”
Passive voice commonly uses be + V3: “The meal is prepared.”
Reported command: told + object + to + V1.

1. Tenses

Definition: Tense is the form of a verb that shows when an action happens—present, past or future.

TenseStructureExample
Simple presentSubject + V1 (s/es)She plays.
Present continuousis/am/are + V-ingShe is playing.
Present perfecthas/have + V3She has played.
Present perfect continuoushas/have been + V-ingShe has been playing.
Simple pastV2She played.
Past continuouswas/were + V-ingShe was playing.
Past perfecthad + V3She had played.
Past perfect continuoushad been + V-ingShe had been playing.
Simple futurewill + V1She will play.
Future continuouswill be + V-ingShe will be playing.
Future perfectwill have + V3She will have played.
Future perfect continuouswill have been + V-ingShe will have been playing.
Verb forms: V1 = go · V2 = went · V3 = gone.

Exam examples

I have not heard from him since last week.
They usually go to the beach every summer.
The children were playing when it started to rain.
The train left five minutes ago.

Remember: “Since” marks a starting point; “for” marks a duration. Words like “usually” often signal simple present; “ago” often signals simple past.

2. Interrogative sentences

Definition: An interrogative sentence asks a question and usually ends with a question mark (?).

Yes/no questions

Auxiliary verb + subject + main verb + …?
You are coming. → Are you coming?
They finished. → Did they finish?

Wh-questions

WordUsed forExample
WhatThing/actionWhat did she say?
WhoPersonWho called you?
WherePlaceWhere do you live?
WhenTimeWhen will you arrive?
WhyReasonWhy are you late?
HowMethod/mannerHow did you do it?
Important: After “did”, use V1: “Did he go?” not “Did he went?”

4. Direct and indirect speech

Definition: Direct speech quotes the exact words spoken. Indirect (reported) speech reports the words without quoting them exactly.

Core steps

  1. Remove quotation marks.
  2. Adjust pronouns to fit the speaker and listener.
  3. Change tense when needed, especially after a past reporting verb.
  4. Adjust time/place words when the context requires it.
DirectReported
“I am happy.”He said that he was happy.
“I have finished.”She said that she had finished.
“I will come.”He said that he would come.
“I can swim.”She said that she could swim.

Commands

told / asked + object + (not) to + V1
The teacher said, “Open your books.” → The teacher told the students to open their books.
He said, “Do not run.” → He told me not to run.

Reported questions

She said, “Where do you live?” → She asked me where I lived.

Reported questions use statement word order: “where I lived,” not “where did I live.”

5. Active and passive voice

Definition: Active voice shows the subject doing the action. Passive voice puts the receiver of the action as the subject.

Active: The chef is preparing the meal.
Passive: The meal is being prepared by the chef.
Basic formula: Object + suitable form of “be” + V3 + (by + doer).
TensePassive formExample
Simple presentis/am/are + V3The room is cleaned.
Present continuousis/am/are being + V3The room is being cleaned.
Present perfecthas/have been + V3The room has been cleaned.
Simple pastwas/were + V3The room was cleaned.
Past continuouswas/were being + V3The room was being cleaned.
Past perfecthad been + V3The room had been cleaned.
Simple futurewill be + V3The room will be cleaned.
Modalmodal + be + V3The room must be cleaned.

Note: Only verbs that take an object can normally be changed into passive voice.

6. Sentence types and transformation

Definition: A sentence is a group of words expressing a complete thought.

Simple sentence

Contains one independent clause.

We stayed indoors.

Compound sentence

Joins two independent clauses, often with and, but, or, so.

It started raining, so we stayed indoors.

Complex sentence

Contains an independent clause and at least one dependent clause.

As it started raining, we stayed indoors.
Common complex-sentence connectors: because, although, when, while, if, since, as.

When transforming sentences, keep the original meaning as close as possible.

Extra grammar: quick reference

Parts of speech

Noun: names a person/place/thing/idea. Pronoun: replaces a noun. Verb: action/state. Adjective: describes a noun. Adverb: modifies a verb/adjective/adverb. Preposition: shows relationship. Conjunction: joins words or clauses. Interjection: expresses sudden feeling.

Articles

A before a consonant sound (a book); an before a vowel sound (an apple, an hour); the for a specific or unique thing (the sun).

Prepositions

At 5 p.m.; on Monday; in October. Interested in, good at, afraid of, depend on, listen to.

Subject–verb agreement

A singular subject generally takes a singular verb: “She plays.” A plural subject generally takes a plural verb: “They play.” After does/did, use V1: “He did not go.”

Modals

Can (ability), may (permission/possibility), must (obligation), should (advice), might (possibility), will (future). A modal is usually followed by V1: “She can sing.”

Conjunctions

And, but, or, so join equal parts; because, although, when and if introduce dependent clauses; either…or and both…and are paired conjunctions.

Degrees of comparison

Positive: tall. Comparative: taller. Superlative: tallest. Good → better → best. Bad → worse → worst.

Question tags

Positive statement + negative tag: “You are ready, aren't you?” Negative statement + positive tag: “He isn't late, is he?”

Conditionals

Zero: If you heat ice, it melts. First: If it rains, I will stay home. Second: If I had money, I would travel. Third: If I had studied, I would have passed.

Gerund and infinitive

Gerund: verb + -ing used as a noun (“Swimming is fun”). Infinitive: to + V1 (“I want to learn”).

Punctuation

Full stop (.), comma (,), question mark (?), exclamation mark (!), colon (:), semicolon (;), quotation marks (“ ”) and apostrophe ('). Capitalise the start of a sentence, proper nouns, days/months and “I”.
